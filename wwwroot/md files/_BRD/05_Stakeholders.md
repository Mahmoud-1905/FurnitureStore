# 5. Stakeholders

Understanding the stakeholders is crucial as it dictates the user roles, system permissions, and overall design priorities of the Ruqi Store System.

## 5.1 External Stakeholders

### Customers (End Users)
- **Profile:** Individuals looking to purchase high-quality furniture. They may range from highly tech-savvy users to those less familiar with e-commerce.
- **Primary Concerns:** Ease of navigation, accurate product imagery and dimensions, trustworthy reviews, seamless checkout, and the ability to easily book an appointment to see the furniture in person.
- **System Value:** The system provides them with convenience and confidence in their purchasing decisions.

## 5.2 Internal Stakeholders

### Store Manager
- **Profile:** The primary operator of the furniture store responsible for day-to-day business activities.
- **Primary Concerns:** Accurate inventory tracking to prevent overselling, fast order processing, manageable appointment schedules without double-booking, and access to sales analytics.
- **System Value:** Consolidates all operational tasks into a single, intuitive dashboard, replacing manual ledgers and phone-based scheduling.

### System Administrator
- **Profile:** IT personnel responsible for the technical health and security of the platform.
- **Primary Concerns:** System uptime, data integrity, user oversight (banning malicious actors), moderating inappropriate content, and monitoring audit logs for security breaches.
- **System Value:** Provides centralized tools to maintain system integrity and enforce platform rules.

## 5.3 Project & Academic Stakeholders

### Development Team
- **Profile:** The students/engineers building the system.
- **Primary Concerns:** Clear requirements, technical feasibility, code maintainability, adhering to the 3-tier architecture, and meeting project deadlines.

### University Supervisor
- **Profile:** The academic evaluator overseeing the graduation project.
- **Primary Concerns:** Completeness of documentation, correct application of software engineering principles, fulfillment of academic constraints (e.g., no frontend frameworks), and overall project quality.
